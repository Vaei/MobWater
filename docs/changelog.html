Changelog

Not officially released. Expect updates without versioning, and a changelog that does not yet reflect them.

1.0.0

Bodies

  • Box and disc pools, closed-spline lakes, open-spline rivers with per-point widths, and an ocean on a ring mesh that follows the view
  • Generated surface meshes, and a spline body that builds its own
  • Extent and depth on the component, so an artist sets a size and never touches a transform
  • Look presets, copied onto a body rather than linked to it: Stylized, Toon and Realistic
  • Wave presets per shape, so a newly placed pond does not arrive carrying an ocean's swell

Surface

  • Depth-graded colour and clarity, both read from the water column recovered from scene depth
  • A gradient fork: the same column indexes a GradientTool ramp instead of an absorption, which is the only way to a hard band. Four palettes ship, the row is per body, and it is a variant so absorption water carries no ramp
  • Minimum opacity, for water that has to read as a flat colour rather than as thin water
  • Unlit, for water whose colour should be the colour that was chosen
  • Two scrolling detail normals and a macro swell, with a drift that is independent of flow and waves
  • Shoreline, edge, crest and ripple foam, banded, separated, noise-broken and scaled by one opacity
  • Foam that suppresses the normal, the glint and the reflection where it is opaque, rather than only tinting them
  • An optional foam texture sampled in the shoreline's own frame, so streaks run away from the bank on every side at once
  • Pixel Normal Offset refraction, as a variant that compiles the scene colour read out entirely
  • Fresnel-weighted sky reflection from a long-latitude texture, with rotation and intensity on the collection
  • Sun glint from a tracked directional light, with gloss, threshold, density and emissive
  • Caustics projected onto the bed, lost with depth

Waves

  • Gerstner waves, up to eight, with speed from the dispersion relation rather than authored
  • A baked FFT sea state for the ocean: a Phillips spectrum solved offline and sampled rather than summed, added on top of the wave preset. It loops exactly, because every component is rounded to a whole number of turns over the period
  • The query reads the sea out of a byte table rather than the texture, so a dedicated server answers the same surface the clients draw
  • The same algorithm on the CPU and in the shader, asserted equal at 192 points across 3 instants
  • Shore attenuation weighted by distance to the body's own edge, computed identically in the vertex shader and the query
  • Waves shoal on what stands in them. An exclusion volume with a shoal distance raises the waves climbing towards it as the fourth root of the run left - Green's law - leans them as it raises them, and collapses them over the last fifteen per cent onto the face. The gain multiplies the choppiness as well as the amplitude, so crest foam whitens at the break without being wired to it
  • Shoaling is independent of strength, so a volume that holds no water back still breaks the sea over itself. A volume with no shoal on it is untouched
  • Run up at the waterline: a crest widens both foam bands and the trough behind it drains them, driven by the wave's fold so a shoaling crest throws the foam further up the rock it is climbing
  • Per-body wave amplitude and wave speed, sharing one data slot, applied inside the wave evaluation so they reach the normal as well as the geometry. The query packs and unpacks the same float the shader reads
  • A supplied clock, folded into a loop period, with the same value reaching the material and every query
  • The baked tier asserted the same way, at 0.05458 cm - a filter weight rather than a difference of maths

Ripples

  • A wave equation on a scrolling 256² render target pair, so ripples spread and reflect off what is standing in them
  • Disturbance components, persistent or moved-triggered, stamped weakest first
  • Wakes, and foam left where something has been
  • One-shot pushes from anywhere, and a clear

Exclusion

  • Disc, sphere, box and rect volumes, evaluated rather than stamped so the edge stays exactly where the geometry is while it moves
  • Four published at once, nearest the view, with the overflow said once in the log
  • A component visualizer to place them by
  • Mesh exclusion: the mesh's own plan view, rasterised once in the editor to a distance mask stored on the component, so a cooked build never reads a vertex buffer. Drawn into a window of its own, and read back by the surface for one tap and no sampler
  • Exclusion subtracted from the CPU query: a fully excluded point answers no water, a partly excluded one has its immersion scaled by what is left, and the amount is reported

Waterfalls

  • A waterfall actor: a spline for the lip, a sheet hung down it, and the churn where it lands. Its own component and its own master material, because every meaning a body of water attaches to its own geometry is a plan view - the ripple field, the shore distance, an exclusion footprint, a wave moving an XY vertex in Z - and none of it survives being stood on end
  • Per point drops, so an uneven ledge over a sloped plunge pool is one actor, and an overhang so a sheet can lean out the way the water is going
  • The sheet is drawn by how long ago the water at a pixel left the lip. The pattern rides down with the parcel it belongs to rather than sliding through it, and it stretches on the way because the same slice of time covers more sheet lower down. That is also why there is no scroll speed: a texture panned at a constant rate reads as a curtain being pulled, and water does not fall at a constant rate
  • The sheet thins and comes apart into strands out of the same arithmetic rather than as separate controls, weighted by how far the water has been drawn out - so a short fall stays whole, a long one breaks up on its own, and a fall told to hang does neither
  • The lip joins to the water feeding it, read at both ends of the line and fitted between them, so a swell crossing the lip does not detach the fall from the river on every wave. Clamped, so a fall placed over the wrong body is visibly wrong in one place rather than dragged off the cliff
  • The base places a persistent disturbance in the ripple field, so the pool below never settles and the churn spreads and reflects the way anything else in that field does
  • Shares the clock, the textures, the sky, the colour ramp and the look preset with the water it comes out of. A fall that does not match its river is worse than no fall
  • Registers no body of water, deliberately: nothing floats on a waterfall, and a buoyancy query that found one would lift whatever asked straight up the cliff
  • Eight instances, which is the whole permutation count - foam, refraction and gradient. There is no ripple field on a vertical sheet, no shoreline to foam against and no bed to throw a caustic on

Surf

  • A surf point component that broadcasts where and when a wave breaks - location, normal, the direction it was aimed, how hard, and how far the water stood over it. It draws nothing: the plume that leaves the water when a wave hits a rock has a life of its own and no heightfield holds one
  • Fires on the crest and rearms as the surface flattens behind it, which paces itself off the sea rather than off a timer
  • Costs one branch with nothing bound to it, and one distance check past Max Distance from a view. mob.Water.Surf 0 stops every one of them
  • Pure CPU, off the analytic model, so two machines given the same instant fire the same impacts
  • The shoal at a point is readable from Blueprint, for a project putting sound or a shove where the water is breaking

Things in it

  • An interaction component: immersion, enter, exit and splash events, ripples from the feet, wade drag and a swim state
  • A buoyancy component: pontoons, a coefficient that is a multiple of the body's own weight, vertical, lateral and angular damping, flow, and an optional alignment to the surface normal
  • A virtual movement seam, because a plugin cannot know what movement component a project uses
  • An underwater component: a plane in front of the camera that absorbs by distance, with the waterline drawn across it as a real line that tilts as a swell passes, a meniscus bead on it, and light dappling down through the water as a compiled permutation
  • The underwater view rides an actor of its own that follows whichever camera is drawing the picture: attached to the local player's camera manager, to the debug camera the moment it takes the local player over, and placed at an editor viewport when nothing is playing at all. It used to be a component on every local controller's camera manager, which failed three ways: a play world draws nothing a hidden actor owns and a camera manager is hidden, an editor viewport has no camera manager to hang it on, and a debug camera left the plane standing where the old view had stopped
  • A query API that answers from the wave model alone, attributed to the object that asked
  • The camera crossing the surface is its own event: UMobWaterSubsystem::OnViewSubmergedChanged, plus the submersion, the depth, and the deepest the camera reached without coming up. Separate from a character's crossing because in anything but a first person game the two happen at different moments in different places, and what belongs to a camera is what happens to a lens
  • Snell's window: the whole world above compressed into the cone the surface lets through, with a bright ring at the critical angle and the water thickened outside it. Its edge is the full Fresnel transmittance reaching zero rather than a fade - Schlick has no critical angle and would leave the rim passing a third of the sky. Filled from the sky the surface already reflects, or from a scene capture of the world above for a level whose shoreline should be in the disc

Editor

  • A Water toolbar menu: draggable placement rows, Set Up Water, Generate Materials, Bake Ocean Spectrum, Verify Contract, Report Cost, Open Wave Collection, Open Selected Body's Material, Select Ocean, Debug Ocean In Rewind Debugger, project settings, and a per-developer hide
  • Set Up Water checks its config file out of source control, so the settings survive a restart
  • A missing-material notification with a link that generates them
  • Details customization that orders the water categories
  • mob.Water.Debug, and mob.Water.Buoyancy for the pontoons

Verification

  • A networked determinism harness: a real dedicated server and two real clients, each recording the surface at eight fixed points every frame, compared row by row. Run again with the clock stepped by a thousand loop periods, which is what proves the fold, and again with two machines a third of a period apart, which must disagree
  • Exclusion checked end to end: real volumes placed, published the way any frame would, and the query compared against what the surface carves
  • Surf held against the physics twice over: the shoal profile and the volume distances compared with Green's law and the break worked longhand on the GPU, then a real reef placed in a real ocean and the answer read back through the same call buoyancy makes - which is what catches a shoal distance that never left the component or a slot published without one
  • The wave parity probe now shoals too, at up to twice open water height, because that is where the steepness clamp decides the answer and a gain applied on the wrong side of it parts the two implementations there and nowhere else
  • The waterfall join asserted rather than eyeballed: a fall placed over a real river, its lip compared with what the query answers at both ends, and the two ends checked to actually differ - which is the only thing that tells a straight fit across the lip from one reading used twice. The same check counts the bodies of water in the world, because a fall registering one would float whatever walked into it up the cliff
  • Snell's window held against Snell's law rather than against a look: every direction a submerged eye can cast, refracted on the GPU and compared with the refracted angle, the Fresnel transmittance and the capture's own projection worked longhand. The disc has no CPU side of its own, so without it the only thing keeping it the right size was that it seemed plausible once

Rewind Debugger

  • A Water track on the world carrying the clock, the wave set and the body count, and a Water Queries track on anything that asked - what it was told and where it asked, which is what tells a water bug from a replication bug
  • A Water Body track on a body of water carrying its surface height, position and yaw. Filed under the actor as well as the component, because what anybody selects is the actor - keyed to the component alone, an ocean picked in the viewport found nothing and read as though nothing had been recorded
  • Water > Debug Ocean In Rewind Debugger, which opens the panel on the level's ocean and deliberately does not start recording
  • Behind the MobWater trace channel, with queries behind a switch of their own. Nothing about ripples or foam is recorded, because a dedicated server never had them

More than one sea state

  • A baked sea state's layout moved off the shared collection and onto the material, so two oceans can carry two different bakes. It used to be one a level, and the second ocean drew the first one's sea at its own scale while answering every query correctly
  • SP_MobWater_Harbour: a second, shorter sea state at a quarter of the resolution and half the loop, shipped so that two in a level is something the plugin's own content does rather than something a page claims

Known gaps

  • A waterfall is a sheet rather than a volume. A torrent thick enough to be looked into is geometry and belongs to the project
  • Its lip join is two readings and a straight line between them, so a lip much longer than the swell crossing it is right at both ends and approximate in the middle
  • A fall makes no hole in the water it lands in. The plunge pushes the field; carving the pool needs an exclusion volume as well
  • A shoaling wave rises, leans and breaks white; it cannot curl over. A Gerstner wave pinches to a cusp and stops, and a heightfield cannot hold two heights over one point. A genuine overturning breaker is a mesh standing on the surface, and belongs to the project
  • Four volumes shoal at once, the nearest to the view, being the four the surface already carves with. A mesh outline cannot shoal at all - its shape is a texture and the vertex shader does not read one
  • The baked sea dies into an obstacle and does not rise against it. Phillips is a deep water sea state, so Green's law is not defined for it
  • A shoal is a plan view distance to a volume rather than the depth of the water, because that is what a vertex shader and a dedicated server can both have. A shoal following a real bed contour has to be approximated with volumes along it
  • The mirror outside Snell's window is not drawn, and the window is refracted through one surface normal, so the disc tilts with the swell but does not ripple
  • A mesh outline reaches only as far as its window does, and four are carried at once
  • The baked sea's normal is sampled at mip zero, because a frame atlas cannot carry a mip chain. Nothing was visible at range beside what the ocean's own detail normals were already doing, but a calmer look may find it
  • Icons are placeholders
