Exclusion

Areas water is kept out of. A hull is dry inside and clear outside, and stays that way while it moves.

Drag a Water Exclusion out of the Water menu, or attach a Mob Water Exclusion component to whatever needs to be dry.

Evaluated, not stamped

An exclusion is geometry: its edge has to be exactly where the hull is. So the nearest few are published to the parameter collection as vectors and the surface evaluates them, rather than being drawn into the ripple field - a field that follows the camera, fades at its border and is quantised to eight centimetres a texel is the wrong place for an edge a boat depends on.

That is the same trade MobLights' fog blockers make, for the same reason. The cost is a hard limit.

Four volumes are rendered at once, the four nearest the view. A fifth holds no water back, and the log says so once. The CPU query answers from every registered volume, not only the four, so a query and a pixel can disagree about a distant hull - the query is right and the pixel is out of slots.

One volume answers both questions - what the surface draws, and what counts as under water - so nothing has to be authored twice to agree. The rendering half is automatic. The submersion half is a number you have to ask for: see below.

It is a footprint, not a volume

Being a plan view, an exclusion has no top and no bottom. Water is kept out of a footprint, not out of a storey. A bridge over a lake does not hold water off its deck, and a cellar under one is dry only if something above it is carving.

This is inherent: the surface is a plane, the mask is a plan view, and giving it a height would mean either a volume texture or a per-pixel test against arbitrary geometry. Neither is affordable here.

Cutting with a mesh

A hull's plan view is not a rectangle and never was. Set Shape to Mesh and the outline is rasterised once, in the editor, into a small mask stored on the component - so a cooked build never reads a vertex buffer and never needs Allow CPUAccess on the mesh.

What the mask holds is distance inside the outline, not coverage. That is what lets Edge Softness mean the same centimetres it means on a box: coverage alone is one bit a texel, and the only edge it could make is however wide one texel happens to be - which changes with the size of the mesh.

The outline is in the mesh's own space, so the exclusion component has to sit where the mesh sits. On an actor whose root is the mesh, that is where it lands by default.

A mesh outline rides the ripple field's spare channel, so it reaches the surface only on a body that reads the field. A body with Ripples off gets the four analytic slots and nothing else. It also reaches only as far as the field does, and fades at its border like everything else in there.
It costs the water nothing to read. The ripple tap already fetches all four channels and only used three of them.

Four outlines are carried at once, largest first, and only ones that actually overlap the field are considered for a slot. They do not compete with the four analytic slots - a mesh never takes one, because carving its bounding rectangle out from under the shape it just cut would undo the whole point.

Moving volumes

An exclusion attached to a moving actor is republished every frame, so a boat carries its hole with it with no smearing and no lag - the values are read by arithmetic in the pixel shader, not resampled from a texture that was drawn last frame.

The four slots are chosen by distance to the view, so a fleet of boats works as long as no more than four are on screen close together. Where more are, the nearest hold their shape and the rest fill in.

Asking on the CPU

const float Excluded = UMobWaterSubsystem::GetExclusionAtLocation(this, Location);

0 is none and 1 is all of it, answered from every registered volume with Blocks Submersion on - the ones that only carve the surface are not in this answer, because nothing has been excluded from the water there, only from the picture of it.

You rarely need to call it. GetWaterInfoAtLocation subtracts exclusion itself: a fully excluded point answers false, and a partly excluded one comes back with its ImmersionDepth scaled by what is left. Scaled rather than switched, because Strength below one is a grating rather than a hull - it thins the water instead of clearing it, and half the water is half the lift.

The amount is reported as Info.Exclusion as well, because a hull that wants to know it is standing in its own well cannot tell that from a body that has gone shallow.

Only volumes with Blocks Submersion on reach the query. One that merely carves the surface has excluded nothing from the water, only from the picture of it.

The two answers are checked against each other

Verify Contract places real volumes, lets the subsystem publish them exactly as it would on any frame, and then asks the surface and the query the same question at a grid of points. The waves are one piece of arithmetic written twice and their probe compares the two; this is not that. What it catches is a break anywhere along the path between them - a slot packed the wrong way round, an outline drawn into the wrong window, a rotation applied on one side only - none of which reading the two implementations would ever show.

Analytic volumes144 points, worst disagreement 0.00001 out of a full exclusion of 1
A baked outline144 points, worst disagreement 0.00067. Looser by design: the query samples the mask directly and the surface reads it back out of an eight bit window, so a value halfway between two of its 255 steps is half a step out however right both sides are
The shape, not its boundsa disc outline is asserted to keep no water out at the corner of its own bounding rectangle. Stated separately because a bounding rectangle agrees with itself perfectly on both sides, so every comparison above would pass while the shape was wrong

A volume waves can feel

Shoal Distance is how far out from a volume a wave feels the ground coming up under it. Approaching it the waves rise, lean, whiten and break against the face. It is independent of Strength, so a volume that holds no water back at all still breaks the sea over itself - which is what a reef or a sandbar is - and a volume with no shoal on it carves exactly as it did before.

The four analytic slots are the four that shoal, so which volumes make surf follows the view the same way the carving does. A mesh outline cannot shoal: its shape is a texture and a vertex shader does not read one. What the waves do about it is in Waves, along with the run up at the waterline and the event a project hangs its spray off.
