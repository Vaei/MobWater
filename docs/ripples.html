Ripples

One camera-following field, stepped by a real wave equation, that every body of water reads.

What it is

A 256² render target pair, its origin snapped to whole texels and kept centred on the view. The step material advances a wave equation across it, scrolls it to follow the camera, and whatever is standing in the water is stamped into it. Bodies with Ripples on read it back as a normal and a foam contribution.

It steps at a fixed sixty a second rather than once a frame, catching up at most two steps after a hitch. Wave speed, damping and how long foam lasts are all per step, so a field stepped per frame is a different body of water on every machine, and the one it happens to run at is the one nobody tuned.

Because it is a wave equation and not a decal that fades, a push spreads outwards, slows nothing down that should not, and reflects off whatever is standing in the way. That is the thing a scrolling texture cannot do at any price.

The cost is fixed by resolution. One disturber and a hundred cost the same field, and a second body of water reading it is not a second cost - only the per-pixel samples that read it are per body, which is what the Ripples variant switches off.

The pushes go in as parameters on the pass that copies the field back, eight to a step, strongest first. That is a character, its wake and a few impacts landing together; past it the weakest push nothing and a warning names the count. Eight is not a budget to spend carefully - a disturber only stamps on the steps it has actually moved on, so a crowd standing in a pond occupies no slots at all.

Reach against fineness

Ripple Extent is how wide the field is; it reaches half of that, from a centre pushed a quarter of its width down the view so a third person camera does not leave its own character at the border. The outer seventh is faded out, so the usable reach is nearer three sevenths of the width than a half - without that fade the field's own border is a straight edge drawn across the water that follows the camera, because still water is a mirror and rippled water is not, and no two shadings of this surface are further apart than those. Two thousand across a 256 target is about eight centimetres a texel, which puts eighteen of them across a character’s ripple. A stamp only nine texels wide reads as a block rather than a ripple. Doubling the reach halves the detail; there is no third option, because the resolution is the cost.

Ripple Speed0.28. How fast a ripple travels, as the square of texels per step - squared because that is the term the equation takes, and writing it as a speed would hide where the limit is. Above 0.5 the equation goes unstable and the field fills with noise that never settles, so it is clamped below it
Ripple Damping0.985. How much survives each step. Below 1 it dies out; at 1 the pond never settles
Ripples Enabledproject wide. Off costs nothing and leaves the field flat

Disturbers

Mob Water Disturbance is a scene component: anything with one pushes the surface where it is.

PropertyDefault
Radius60 cmhow far across the surface it pushes
Strength1.0how far it displaces the surface. Negative pulls it down, which is what an object entering does. Two is as far as the field can hold, and a crest that reaches it is clipped flat
Persistentoffon, it disturbs every frame - a fountain, an outflow. Off is what a character wants: standing still leaves the water to settle, which is what still water does
Foam0.35how white its wake gets. A ceiling, not an amount: stamps overlap by most of their radius, so foam that accumulated would reach solid white within a few strides whatever this was set to, and solid white has no shape in it
Move Threshold0, meaning half the radiushow far it has to move before it stamps again. Shorter than the radius and it pushes water it has already pushed before that push has moved, so a trail of ripples becomes a mound that grows for as long as it keeps walking
Strength is a height. The surface is displaced and let go, which is why the crest that leaves is the size that was asked for - a stamp that reached only the height and not the height a step ago would be a velocity instead, and would keep climbing until it railed.

One-shot pushes

For something that happens rather than something that stands there - an impact, a footfall, a thrown stone:

UMobWaterSubsystem::AddRipple(this, Location, Radius, Strength);

Queued and drawn on the next field tick. ClearRipples() empties the field back to still, which is what a level transition or a cutscene start wants.

Wakes and foam

Anything that keeps moving leaves a wake, because each stamp is a fresh push into a field that is still carrying the last one. Foam goes into the field's third channel up to the disturber's own Foam, at the point it stamped, and only dries from there. Bodies with Foam on read it as one of the four foam sources, so a boat leaves white water behind it without anything being authored.

Foam is put in where the thing was, not worked out from how fast the surface is moving. A ripple carries its crest outwards across the whole body, so foam taken from the surface's motion is a plume spreading ahead of whatever made it rather than a trail behind.

Where several overlap their pushes sum, which is what makes two wakes crossing read as two wakes crossing.

The field is never authoritative

Ripples, wakes and stamped foam live in a render target. A dedicated server has no GPU to hold one, so the CPU query does not know about them and never will. GetWaterInfoAtLocation answers from the wave model alone.

This is a deliberate line, not a limitation to be worked around. Waves are shared because they are a pure function of position and time; ripples are decoration because they are a simulation with state. Building buoyancy on a value only the client can see is how two machines end up disagreeing about where a boat is, with nothing in either log to say why.

If something needs to bob on a passing wake, drive it from the same event that made the wake.
