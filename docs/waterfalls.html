Waterfalls

A sheet hung down a drop, joined to the river above it and churning the pool below.

Not a shape of water

A waterfall is its own actor, its own component and its own material. That is not tidiness - it is the only arrangement that works, and it is worth knowing why before building on it.

Every meaning a body of water attaches to its own geometry is a plan view. The ripple field is looked down on. The shore distance is measured across the ground. An exclusion volume is a footprint. A Gerstner wave moves an XY vertex in Z. Stand any of that on end and it stops meaning anything: a wave on a vertical sheet pushes water sideways out of the fall, and the distance to the shore is the distance to nothing.

So what is shared is what is genuinely shared - the clock, the normal and foam textures, the sky, the colour ramp, and the look preset. A fall that does not match the river it comes out of is worse than no fall at all.

A waterfall does not register as a body of water. Nothing floats on one, and a buoyancy query that found a fall would answer with a surface standing on end and lift whatever asked straight up the cliff. A character standing in the plunge is in whatever pool is underneath it.

One number the whole thing rests on

How long ago the water at a pixel left the lip.

Everything a fall looks like comes off that. The water leaves at Lip Speed and accelerates under Gravity, so a parcel that left a moment ago is above one that left before it and travelling slower. Mark the sheet by which parcel a pixel belongs to and the pattern rides down with the water rather than sliding through it - and it stretches on the way, because the same slice of time covers more sheet lower down.

That is also why there is no scroll speed here. A texture panned at a constant rate over a fall reads as a curtain being pulled, and no amount of tuning fixes it, because water does not fall at a constant rate.

Two more things fall out of the same arithmetic rather than being separate features:

  • The sheet thins. The same water passing a faster section has to be thinner in it. That is what puts the weight at the top of a fall, and Thin Amount is how much of it to keep - 0 holds full thickness all the way down, which reads as a printed curtain.
  • It comes apart. Breakup is weighted by how far the water has been drawn out, not by how far down it is, so a short fall stays whole and a long one breaks into strands on its own. A fall told to hang breaks up nowhere, which is correct: what tears a sheet is the water below outrunning the water above.
Set Gravity to zero for water that hangs. It never stretches, so it never thins and never breaks up, and the pattern simply pans. That is a stylized fall rather than a slow one, and it is a legal thing to ask for.

The two joins

The work in a waterfall is not the sheet. It is the two places the sheet meets water that already exists, and both are easy to leave until they are expensive.

The lip

With Join To Water on, the fall reads the water at each end of its lip - the same query buoyancy makes - and moves the top rows onto whatever surface is actually there. Without it a swell passing the lip detaches the fall from the river on every wave.

Two readings rather than one, because a lip is a line and a wave reaches one end of it before the other. A single offset would hold the join in the middle and open it at both sides. Two is a straight fit across the lip: exact wherever the lip is shorter than the wave crossing it, and closer than nothing wherever it is not.

Join To Wateronoff is right for a fall coming out of a pipe or a rock face, where there is no river above it to follow

The plunge

The base places a persistent disturbance in the ripple field, so the pool below never settles and the churn spreads out of it and reflects off whatever it meets - because the field is a wave equation rather than a decal that fades. Turn Plunge off for a fall landing on rock, where a churn in a pool that is not there is a ripple in mid air.

One disturbance rather than a row of them. Stamps add where they overlap, so a line of them along the base builds a mound instead of a churn; its radius comes from how wide the lip is.

Foam, and how opaque it is

Three sources, because they are three different things and a fall missing any of them reads wrong. Lip Foam is where the sheet breaks over the edge, Base Foam is the plunge, and the streaks between them are the aeration being carried down - which is why they appear where the water has been drawn out, so a slow fat fall stays clear and a long one is white by the time it lands.

The strands and the foam read one noise, and that is not a saving. The strands are where the water is and the froth rides on them; a second noise would put the foam in the gaps.

Opacity answers two questions at once and they are separate. The stretch decides how much water there is. The depth column decides how near the pixel is to whatever it is running over, so the sheet meets rock and the pool on a soft line rather than on the polygon edge where they intersect - Clarity Depth is that softness, and Min Opacity is how solid the sheet stays where it is pressed against something.

Spray

The plume that leaves the water where a fall lands is not a surface. It has a life of its own, and no sheet holds one. MobWater draws none of it: bind the plunge to whatever Niagara a project has, or hang it off the surf event the same way a wave breaking on a rock is.

What a waterfall cannot do here

Worth knowing before building on it, because each of these is a property of the model rather than a gap someone forgot to fill.

  • It is a sheet, not a volume. The material draws one surface. A fall thick enough to be looked into - a torrent with water visibly rolling inside it - is geometry, and belongs to the project.
  • The lip is fitted, not sampled. Two readings and a straight line between them. A lip much longer than the swell crossing it has a join that is right at both ends and approximate in the middle. The fix is more falls along the ledge, which is also what the drop wants.
  • Nothing floats on it and nothing is in it. It registers no body, so queries, wading and swimming, buoyancy and the underwater view all pass straight through. Put a pool at the bottom.
  • It makes no hole in the water it lands in. The plunge pushes the field; it does not carve the pool. A fall that should displace the surface it lands in needs an exclusion volume there as well.
  • One plunge to a fall. Its radius follows the lip’s length, so a very wide fall pushes one wide, soft area rather than a line of separate impacts. Two falls side by side is the way to get two.
  • It does not read the river’s flow. Lip Speed is authored rather than taken from the body above, because a body’s flow defaults to nothing and a fall that inherited it would be dead on most rivers. Set the two to match.
  • The sheet does not know what is behind it. It reads the depth buffer for how near it is to the rock, and nothing else - so it cannot part around an outcrop or run faster where the face steepens. Shape those with the lip and the drops.

Asserted, not eyeballed

Water → Verify Contract places a fall over a real river and reads the join back. The lip has to sit on the water the query answers at both ends, and those two have to differ - which is the only thing that tells a straight fit across the lip from one reading used twice. It also counts the bodies of water in the world before and after, because a fall that registered one would float whatever walked into it up the cliff.

The last run: the lip sat on its river to 0.0000 cm with the ends 5.97 cm apart, and the fall registered no body.

Cost

Its own master and eight instances, which is the whole permutation count: Foam, Refraction and Gradient, and nothing else. There is no ripple field on a vertical sheet, no shoreline to foam against, and no bed under it to throw a caustic on, so none of those axes exist here.

The measured figures are on the cost page beside the surface’s. The vertex count is small - a fall displaces only to meet its lip - and the pixel count is most of it.
